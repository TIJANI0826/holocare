Diabetes Mellitus

What is Diabetes?

Diabetes is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by elevated blood glucose levels resulting from insufficient insulin production or ineffective insulin utilization. In Nigeria, diabetes affects over 3.2 million people, with an estimated prevalence of 3.1% of the adult population.

Types of Diabetes
  • Type 1: Autoimmune condition requiring insulin therapy
  • Type 2: Most common (90% of cases), often preventable through lifestyle
  • Gestational: Occurs during pregnancy, increases lifelong risk
Symptoms to Watch For
  • ✓ Increased thirst and frequent urination
  • ✓ Unexplained weight loss
  • ✓ Persistent fatigue and weakness
  • ✓ Blurred vision
  • ✓ Slow-healing wounds or frequent infections
Management Strategies
  • ✓ Regular blood glucose monitoring
  • ✓ Balanced diet with complex carbohydrates
  • ✓ Regular physical exercise (150 mins/week)
  • ✓ Medication adherence as prescribed
  • ✓ Annual comprehensive health screening

Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)

Understanding Hypertension

Hypertension is the persistent elevation of systemic arterial blood pressure above 140/90 mmHg. It is often termed "the silent killer" because many individuals remain asymptomatic until serious complications develop. Nigeria has one of the highest hypertension burdens in Africa, with approximately 30% of adults affected.

Risk Factors
  • • Family history of hypertension
  • • Age above 60 years
  • • Obesity and overweight
  • • Excessive sodium intake
  • • Stress and sedentary lifestyle
Possible Symptoms
  • → Headaches (often at the back of head)
  • → Dizziness or lightheadedness
  • → Shortness of breath
  • → Nosebleeds (in severe cases)
  • → Chest discomfort
Holcare Management Approach
  • ✓ Regular blood pressure monitoring
  • ✓ DASH diet (rich in potassium, magnesium)
  • ✓ Reduced salt intake (<5g/day)
  • ✓ Stress management and relaxation techniques
  • ✓ Prescribed medications with functional medicine

Stroke (Cerebrovascular Accident)

What Happens During a Stroke?

A stroke occurs when blood flow to part of the brain is suddenly interrupted, depriving brain cells of oxygen. This results in rapid death of brain tissue and potential permanent disability. In Nigeria, stroke is among the leading causes of morbidity and mortality, with an estimated incidence of 112 per 100,000 populations annually.

Types of Stroke
  • Ischemic (80%): Blood clot blocks blood vessel
  • Hemorrhagic (20%): Blood vessel ruptures
  • TIA: Temporary blockage (warning sign)

Prevention Strategies
  • ✓ Control blood pressure
  • ✓ Manage diabetes and cholesterol
  • ✓ Avoid smoking and alcohol
  • ✓ Regular physical activity
  • ✓ Heart-healthy diet
Post-Stroke Recovery
  • ✓ Early rehabilitation therapy
  • ✓ HBOT for tissue recovery
  • ✓ Physical and speech therapy
  • ✓ Medication management
  • ✓ Psychological support services

Chronic Liver Disease

Overview

Chronic liver disease encompasses a range of disorders affecting liver function over prolonged periods. Common causes include hepatitis B and C, alcohol abuse, fatty liver disease, and autoimmune conditions. Nigeria faces significant burden from viral hepatitis, with approximately 8% hepatitis B and 2.1% hepatitis C seroprevalence rates.

Common Causes
  • • Viral hepatitis (B, C, A)
  • • Alcohol-related liver disease
  • • Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease
  • • Autoimmune hepatitis
  • • Cirrhosis (advanced stage)
Warning Signs
  • → Jaundice (yellowing of skin/eyes)
  • → Abdominal swelling or pain
  • → Persistent fatigue
  • → Dark urine or pale stools
  • → Easy bruising or bleeding
Management Approach
  • ✓ Regular liver function testing
  • ✓ Hepatitis screening and vaccination
  • ✓ Alcohol cessation
  • ✓ Nutritional optimization
  • ✓ Antiviral therapy when indicated

Insulin Resistance

What is Insulin Resistance?

Insulin resistance happens when the body's cells become less responsive to insulin, forcing the pancreas to produce more of it. This leads to higher blood sugar levels, metabolic strain, and increased risk for type 2 diabetes.

Why It Matters
  • • Higher risk of diabetes and heart disease
  • • Often linked to excess abdominal fat
  • • Can cause fatigue, cravings, and brain fog
Signs to Watch For
  • → Persistent sugar cravings
  • → Difficulty losing weight
  • → High blood pressure or cholesterol
  • → Darkened patches of skin around the neck or underarms
Holcare Management
  • ✓ Personalized low-glycemic nutrition
  • ✓ Regular exercise to improve insulin sensitivity
  • ✓ Stress reduction and better sleep
  • ✓ Weight management and metabolic coaching
Support from Holcare

Our team combines functional medicine, nutrition, and lifestyle support to help reverse insulin resistance and reduce the risk of diabetes-related complications.

Non Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease

What is NAFLD?

NAFLD is the buildup of fat in the liver in people who drink little or no alcohol. It is strongly linked to metabolic conditions such as insulin resistance, obesity, and type 2 diabetes.

Major Risk Factors
  • • Overweight or obesity
  • • Insulin resistance
  • • High triglycerides or low HDL cholesterol
  • • Poor diet high in processed foods
Signs and Symptoms
  • → Often no symptoms early on
  • → Mild pain or discomfort in the upper right abdomen
  • → Fatigue and weakness
  • → Elevated liver enzymes during blood tests
Holcare Support
  • ✓ Tailored dietary plans for liver health
  • ✓ Exercise and weight-management coaching
  • ✓ Monitoring of liver function and metabolic markers
  • ✓ Inflammation reduction through functional therapies
Why Early Action Matters

Early identification and lifestyle intervention can stop NAFLD from progressing to non alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), fibrosis, or cirrhosis.

Chronic Kidney Disease

Understanding CKD

Chronic Kidney Disease is characterized by progressive loss of kidney function over months or years. The kidneys filter waste products and excess water from the blood, which are then excreted as urine. When kidney function declines to less than 10% of normal, dialysis or transplantation becomes necessary. Nigeria has a high burden of CKD, with diabetes and hypertension being primary causes.

Primary Causes
  • • Diabetes (leading cause)
  • • Hypertension
  • • Chronic glomerulonephritis
  • • Recurrent kidney infections
  • • Obstructive diseases
Early Symptoms
  • → Changes in urination patterns
  • → Swelling in legs, ankles, feet
  • → Persistent back or side pain
  • → Fatigue and weakness
  • → Shortness of breath
Holcare Treatment Plan
  • ✓ Regular renal function monitoring
  • ✓ Blood pressure control
  • ✓ Dietary modifications (sodium, protein)
  • ✓ Medication management
  • ✓ Functional nutrition support
